## Rationale
P3099, "User-Defined Diagnostic Messages" -- **approved by EWG**.  A
contract may carry a message, delivered to the violation handler as
`message()`.

**This commit is eight lines of source and nineteen test files, and the
disproportion is the thing to understand about it.**  The machinery it
gates is already present in earlier commits and could not be separated
from them: the `HasMessage` slot and `getUserMessage` are part of
`ContractStmt` (`1020-p2900-base-ast`), the `[[clang::contract_message]]`
attribute handler rides with the other statement attributes
(`2400-p3595`), the `message` field is one of the ABI's extensible fields
(`2200-libcontracts`), and `contract_violation::message()` is declared
alongside the other accessors (`1160-p2900-base-libcxx`).  A net diff
carries only the final text of each of those, so there is no cut that
recovers a pre-P3099 version of any of them -- 3.5(a), applied to a paper
rather than to a bug fix.

What is genuinely P3099's, then, is the decision to expose it: the
`-fcontracts-p3099` flag and `__cpp_contracts_message`.  Dropping this
commit leaves every one of those pieces compiled in and unreachable.

The one design point recorded in the code rather than here:
`message()` returns `nullptr` for a contract with no message, where
`comment()` returns `""`.  P3099's "Option C1" keeps "no message supplied"
distinct from "an empty message", and the comment in `<contracts>` says so
-- worth knowing before deciding one of them is a bug.

The tests are where the paper actually lives: constant-expression messages,
class-typed messages, messages on `contract_assert`, escapes, the empty
message, the null message, the redeclaration rules, and the interaction
with P3290's `assert` integration, which has a message of its own.

## Compile gap
Needs `1020-p2900-base-ast`, `1160-p2900-base-libcxx`, `2200-libcontracts`
and `2400-p3595` -- all of which precede it, and all of which carry a piece
of the facility this commit turns on.

Nothing to stub in either direction.  This is a gate, and both sides of it
already exist.

**Behaviour trap, and it is this commit's defining property:** with the
flag off, everything still compiles and the message is simply never
surfaced.  A test that supplies a message and checks that the program
aborts passes identically with and without this commit.  Which is why
nearly every test here reads the message *back* through the handler rather
than asserting on the exit status.
